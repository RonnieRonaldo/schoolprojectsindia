What Is in the Traffic Rules Model?

The prototype includes a road with a zebra crossing, pavements and lane markings; two paper children; a red car; traffic signs; small plants; and a paper traffic light. The light uses paper pieces, not electronics.

How to Make the Paper Road Safety Model

These photographs show how the prototype was made. Once the PDF is available, use its instructions alongside the pictures. Prepare a printer, paper, scissors, glue and backing card. Younger children may need adult help with cutting.

1

Prepare the Base

Print the road sheet, trim it and attach it to backing card to keep it flat.

2

Cut and Fold

Cut out the figures, car, signs and traffic light parts. Fold the marked tabs.

3

Build and Discuss

Assemble the light and arrange the pieces around the road. Use the scene to start a conversation about traffic rules.

Use the Model for a Traffic Awareness Activity

Let children point out the traffic light, zebra crossing, pavement and signs. Ask them what each feature is for and let them move the paper pieces to explain their ideas.

  • Can you name the three colours on the traffic light?
  • Which part of the model is for people walking?
  • What do you notice about the signs near the crossing?
  • How would you explain this model to your class?

Parents and teachers can guide the discussion using local road safety teaching materials. The paper scene is a simplified classroom model, not a complete guide to crossing a real road.

Questions About the Free PDF

Can I download the PDF now?

Not yet. The file is still being prepared. Bookmark this page and check back for the download link.

Will the complete kit be free?

The planned experimental PDF will be offered free through a temporary link on this page. It will include the road base, cut-out pieces and instructions together.

Does the traffic light work electronically?

No. It is a paper traffic light model made from a housing and a coloured strip. No batteries, LEDs or electronic parts are included.

Is this the final design?

No. The artwork and appearance are still being refined. The photographs show the current prototype, and we welcome feedback.

Can it be used for a school assignment?

It is intended as a hands-on traffic rules and road safety activity. Check your teacher’s requirements before choosing it for a school assignment.
