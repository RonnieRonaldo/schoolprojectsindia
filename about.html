Hello, I’m Ronald

I design printable paper projects that turn a few sheets and everyday craft materials into small worlds you can build with your hands.

From an idea to a paper world

I enjoy the whole process: imagining a scene, drawing its pieces, printing, cutting and finding out how everything fits. There are usually scraps, test pieces and another idea waiting on my worktable.

A design does not always work the first time. Moving a piece, adjusting a fold and trying again are part of the fun. My collection grew from that curiosity into dioramas, miniature scenes and printable templates that I also share through Etsy.

Space for children’s own ideas

Making things for and with my children has shaped the way I think about a project. A template is a starting point. Children can change where the animals stand, add their own scenery or invent a story around the finished model.

Family craft time does not have to mean completing everything in one sitting. Prepare the pages, cut a few pieces and come back to the scene later. It is satisfying to see flat paper gradually become something you can hold.

A simpler start for busy parents

You should not need to design every tree, animal and background before your child can start building. My printable pieces provide that starting point, while leaving the cutting, arranging and creating to your family.

Most projects use paper, scissors and glue, with a shoebox or cardboard base where the model requires it. You supply the materials and print the PDF at home or at a print shop. Check each project for its specific requirements.

School projects and learning at home

School Projects India brings these designs together for families looking for EVS project ideas, habitat displays and paper science models. If your child has holiday homework or a school exhibition assignment, start with the teacher’s instructions and choose a project that fits.

Building an ocean habitat or an Arctic diorama can also open up a conversation during learning at home. Ask questions, look things up together and let your child explain their discoveries. These are independent craft resources, not a prescribed school course.

What will you build?

I hope these projects make getting started easier and leave plenty of room for imagination. A finished model is lovely, but the choices, questions and little discoveries along the way are just as much a part of it.
