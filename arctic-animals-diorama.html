Take a Closer Look at the Arctic Diorama

The upright backdrop and standing animal cutouts create depth inside the box. Arrange the animals across the scene so children can point to them while describing their project.

Arctic Animals to Discover

The cutouts give children a starting point for learning about polar animals and their habitats. This is a creative display bringing several Arctic animals together; in nature, they use different habitats and do not all live on the same patch of ice.

Polar Bears and Sea Ice

Polar bears use sea ice as a platform for hunting seals. Use the bear cutouts to introduce polar bear habitat and discuss why sea ice matters to them.

Walruses and the Ocean

Walruses feed in the sea and rest on ice or land. Their long tusks and whiskers make them easy to recognise in the display.

Narwhals and Orcas

Narwhals live in Arctic waters. Orcas live in many parts of the world's oceans, including Arctic waters. Both are marine mammals that breathe air.

Reindeer and Snowy Owls

Reindeer and snowy owls connect the project to Arctic land habitats. They are useful examples when discussing how the tundra differs from the sea-ice environment.

How to Make an Arctic Shoebox Diorama

Prepare a shoebox, printer, paper, scissors and glue. Cardboard can help reinforce any parts that need extra support. Younger children may need an adult's help with cutting.

1

Print and Prepare

Print the PDF, look through the assembly guidance and identify the scenery and animal pieces.

2

Cut, Fold and Glue

Cut out the pieces, keep the tabs and fold them as shown. Attach the scenery to your box.

3

Arrange and Explain

Position the animals in the scene. Prepare a few sentences about the Arctic and the animals you have included.

Ideas for Your School Presentation

Once the model is ready, use these questions to prepare your own explanation:

  • Where is the Arctic region on a globe?
  • Which animals in your diorama spend time in the sea?
  • Why is sea ice important for polar bears?
  • How do thick fur or a layer of fat help some animals stay warm?
  • How is the Arctic different from Antarctica?

These are discussion ideas for this webpage. Children can research the answers and make their own labels or presentation cards.
