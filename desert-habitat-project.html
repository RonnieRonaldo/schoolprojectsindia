Desert Habitat Project for Kids

Need a desert habitat project for school? This printable 3D model helps children create a colourful desert scene with camels, cactus plants, desert animals, rocks and a tent for EVS homework, habitat assignments and school projects.

An Easy Desert Project for Busy Parents

If your child suddenly needs a desert habitat model, camel habitat project or EVS assignment for school, starting from an empty box can take more time than expected. This printable project gives you the main scenery, animals and desert elements so the model already has a clear structure.

Children can still make the project their own by deciding where the camels, cactus plants, tent, rocks and desert animals should go. Extra sand, stones or craft materials can also be added at home.

What Children Can Learn

A desert habitat model is a simple visual way to learn how animals and plants survive in hot and dry environments. Children can explore desert animals, camels, cactus plants, water scarcity and the different features of a desert ecosystem.

The finished model can be used for EVS projects, habitat lessons, ecosystem activities, environmental studies, homework and school exhibitions.

See What Is Included

The printable pages include desert backgrounds, ground sections, camels, cactus plants, rocks, a colourful tent and other scenery pieces for building a layered 3D desert model.

Printable parts used to build the desert habitat school project.

Build a 3D Desert Scene

Instead of making only a flat poster, children can arrange the printable pieces at different depths inside a shoebox or cardboard box. This gives the desert habitat a much stronger 3D effect and makes it easier to see where different plants and animals live.

How to Make the Desert Habitat Project

1

Print

Print the desert project pages at home on A4 paper or slightly thicker paper.

2

Cut

Cut out the camels, cactus plants, tent, rocks and other desert pieces.

3

Build

Arrange and glue the pieces inside a shoebox or similar cardboard box to create the 3D model.

See the Finished Desert Model

The finished project combines a large desert landscape with smaller 3D elements such as camels, plants, animals, rocks and a tent. Children can move the pieces around before gluing them down and create their own version of the habitat.
